AWARDS
Pan's Labyrinth (2006, dir. Guillermo del Toro) won three Academy Awards at the 79th Academy Awards: Best Art Direction (Eugenio Caballero, Pilar Revuelta), Best Cinematography (Guillermo Navarro) and Best Makeup (David Martí, Montse Ribé).
Pan's Labyrinth also won three BAFTA Awards, including Best Film Not in the English Language, plus the Hugo Award for Best Dramatic Presentation, Long Form; it grossed about $83 million worldwide.
Pan's Labyrinth (Mexico, Spanish-language) was also nominated at the 79th Academy Awards for Best Foreign Language Film, Best Original Screenplay (del Toro) and Best Original Score (Javier Navarrete), but won only its three technical awards.
Sweeney Todd: The Demon Barber of Fleet Street (2007, dir. Tim Burton) won the Golden Globe for Best Motion Picture - Musical or Comedy and Johnny Depp won the Golden Globe for Best Actor - Musical or Comedy at the 65th Golden Globes.
Sweeney Todd received three Oscar nominations at the 80th Academy Awards (Best Actor for Johnny Depp, Costume Design, Art Direction) and won Best Art Direction.
Saw (2004) was nominated for Best Horror Film at the 31st Saturn Awards.
BOX OFFICE AND RECORDS
I Am Legend (2007, dir. Francis Lawrence, starring Will Smith) grossed $585,532,684 worldwide, ranking No. 3 on Wikipedia's list of highest-grossing horror films (behind It (2017) and The Sixth Sense (1999)) - the highest of any 2000s horror release.
I Am Legend opened to $77.2 million in December 2007, the highest December opening weekend at the time, beating The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King, until The Hobbit: An Unexpected Journey (2012).
Among 2000s releases on Wikipedia's highest-grossing horror list, M. Night Shyamalan's Signs (2002) grossed $408,250,578 worldwide (No. 11), Hannibal (2001) $351,692,268 (No. 16) and Van Helsing (2004) $300,157,638 (No. 25).
Paranormal Activity (shot for $15,000 by Oren Peli) had a limited US release on September 25, 2009 and a nationwide release on October 16, 2009; it grossed $194,183,034 worldwide and is often cited as the most profitable film ever by return on investment.
Paramount acquired the Paranormal Activity US rights from Peli for $350,000 and shot a new ending costing an additional $200,000; in its first full wide weekend it took No. 1 with $21.1 million from 1,945 theaters, beating Saw VI.
Guinness named Saw 'the most successful horror franchise in history' in 2010.
FRANCHISES AND REMAKES
The Saw films were released every October in the US: Saw (Oct 29, 2004, dir. James Wan), Saw II (Oct 28, 2005, Darren Lynn Bousman), Saw III (Oct 27, 2006), Saw IV (Oct 26, 2007), Saw V (Oct 24, 2008, David Hackl), Saw VI (Oct 23, 2009, Kevin Greutert).
Saw was created by James Wan and Leigh Whannell, premiered at Sundance on January 19, 2004 before its October 29, 2004 release by Lions Gate, and follows serial killer John 'Jigsaw' Kramer (Tobin Bell).
Final Destination (2000) was based on an unused spec script by Jeffrey Reddick originally written for The X-Files; the 2000s entries are Final Destination (2000), Final Destination 2 (2003), Final Destination 3 (2006) and The Final Destination (2009, dir. David R. Ellis).
The Resident Evil films, based on Capcom's games, star Milla Jovovich as the film-original Alice: Resident Evil (2002, Paul W. S. Anderson), Apocalypse (2004, Alexander Witt) and Extinction (2007, Russell Mulcahy).
Hostel (2005) was written and directed by Eli Roth and executive produced by Quentin Tarantino among others; it follows American tourists in Slovakia.
DEBUTS AND CASTING
James Wan directed Saw (2004) in his feature directorial debut from a script by Leigh Whannell, who also co-starred alongside Cary Elwes; after failing to get the script made in Australia, they shot a short film of the same name to attract producers.
Saw was shot in 18 days on a budget of about $1–1.2 million and went on to gross $104 million worldwide after Lions Gate picked it up following its January 2004 Sundance screening.
Nick Frost made his film debut in Shaun of the Dead (2004), a film inspired by an episode of Edgar Wright and Simon Pegg's sitcom Spaced; Pegg and Wright co-wrote it.
Zack Snyder made his feature directorial debut with the 2004 Dawn of the Dead remake, written by James Gunn, with original-film actors Scott Reiniger, Tom Savini and Ken Foree in cameos.
T.J. Miller made his film debut in Cloverfield (2008), directed by Matt Reeves and written by Drew Goddard.
BEHIND THE SCENES
Paranormal Activity (2007) was shot for $15,000; DreamWorks then bought it and added a new ending costing $200,000, and it grossed about $194 million worldwide.
Paranormal Activity had no real script: Oren Peli gave the actors story outlines to improvise, a 'retroscripting' technique also used on The Blair Witch Project, and he auditioned 'a few hundred people' before choosing Katie Featherston and Micah Sloat.
Cloverfield (2008) had the working titles Slusho, Cheese and Greyshot, was secretly greenlit by Paramount in February 2007, and earned $172.4 million against a $25 million budget; its first teaser ran untitled before Transformers (2007).
Cloverfield producer J. J. Abrams conceived it after seeing Godzilla toys in Japan, wanting 'our own American monster'; the creature was designed by Neville Page.

INTERNATIONAL HORROR
Ju-On: The Grudge (2002) by Takashi Shimizu was the third Ju-On installment and the first theatrical one; Shimizu remade it in English as The Grudge (2004) with Sarah Michelle Gellar, which grossed $187.3 million.
Kiyoshi Kurosawa's Kairo (Pulse, 2001) premiered in Un Certain Regard at Cannes; an English-language remake, Pulse, followed in 2006.
Hideo Nakata's Dark Water (2002), whose original Japanese title echoes Koji Suzuki's anthology From the Depths of Dark Water, was remade in English in 2005 by Walter Salles with Jennifer Connelly.
Kim Jee-woon's A Tale of Two Sisters (2003) is the highest-grossing South Korean horror film and was remade as The Uninvited (2009); it is based on the Joseon-era folktale Janghwa Hongryeon jeon.
Bong Joon-ho's The Host (2006), about a Han River monster, debuted at Cannes' Directors' Fortnight on May 21, 2006, and became South Korea's highest-grossing film of all time; it was inspired by the 2000 McFarland formaldehyde incident.
