POSTSEASON HISTORY
Don Larsen of the New York Yankees threw a perfect game in Game 5 of the 1956 World Series on October 8, 1956, beating the Brooklyn Dodgers 2-0 with Yogi Berra catching. It remains the only perfect game in World Series history; he struck out seven and was named Series MVP.
Roy Halladay of the Phillies threw a no-hitter against the Cincinnati Reds in Game 1 of the 2010 NLDS on October 6, 2010 (4-0), and is the only pitcher with no-hitters in both the regular season (his May 29, 2010 perfect game) and postseason.
Bill Mazeroski hit a ninth-inning home run in Game 7 of the 1960 World Series to give the Pittsburgh Pirates the title over the Yankees; it was the first World Series to end on a home run and the first winner-take-all World Series game to end that way.
The 1960 Yankees outscored the Pirates but lost: they scored 55 runs, the most by any team in a World Series, yet Pittsburgh won 4-3. Bobby Richardson was MVP, the first non-pitcher MVP and the only MVP from a losing team.
Carlton Fisk's walk-off home run won Game 6 of the 1975 World Series for the Red Sox in the 12th inning (7-6 over the Reds) at Fenway Park; the Reds then won Game 7 on a ninth-inning Joe Morgan single.
Kirk Gibson, injured in both legs and not starting, hit a pinch-hit walk-off home run off Oakland closer Dennis Eckersley in Game 1 of the 1988 World Series; the Dodgers won in five games and Orel Hershiser was MVP.
Orel Hershiser set the all-time scoreless-inning streak at 59 innings in the 1988 regular season, then won the Series MVP.
Joe Carter hit a walk-off three-run home run in the bottom of the ninth of Game 6 of the 1993 World Series, with Toronto trailing the Phillies 6-5, to clinch the Blue Jays' second straight title. It was only the second World Series ended by a home run (after Mazeroski in 1960).
In Game 6 of the 1986 World Series the Mets rallied with two outs and nobody on in the bottom of the 10th, won on an error by Red Sox first baseman Bill Buckner, with Boston twice one strike from the title; the Mets then won Game 7. The collapse became part of the Curse of the Bambino lore.
David Freese hit a game-tying two-run triple in the ninth and a walk-off home run in the 11th of Game 6 of the 2011 World Series (Cardinals 10, Rangers 9); St. Louis twice came back from two runs down, each time with Texas one strike from a title. Freese was Series MVP and the Cardinals won in seven.
Albert Pujols hit three home runs in Game 3 of the 2011 World Series, a feat previously done only by Reggie Jackson and Babe Ruth and later by Pablo Sandoval in 2012.
Game 3 of the 2018 World Series lasted 18 innings and 7 hours 20 minutes, the longest World Series game by innings and time: Max Muncy homered in the bottom of the 18th to give the Dodgers a 3-2 win over the Red Sox (October 26, 2018). The Red Sox still won the Series 4-1.
Game 3 of the 2025 World Series also went 18 innings: Freddie Freeman hit a walk-off home run off Brendon Little in the 18th for the Dodgers over the Blue Jays; it was the second-longest World Series game by time.
The 2025 World Series: the Los Angeles Dodgers beat the Toronto Blue Jays 4-3, winning Game 7 in 11 innings (Miguel Rojas tied it with a two-out ninth-inning homer, Will Smith hit the go-ahead homer in the 11th). Yoshinobu Yamamoto was Series MVP. Dodgers were the first back-to-back champion since the 2000 Yankees.
Reggie Jackson hit three consecutive home runs in the clinching Game 6 of the 1977 World Series at Yankee Stadium (five home runs in the Series) and was MVP; this is the root of his 'Mr. October' nickname.
The Chicago Cubs won the 2016 World Series 4-3 over Cleveland, winning Game 7 8-7 in ten innings after a rain delay and coming back from a 3-1 deficit; it was their first title since 1908 and ended what Wikipedia calls the longest championship drought in North American pro sports history.
The Curse of the Bambino was the superstition behind the Red Sox's 86-year drought (1918-2004), tied to Harry Frazee selling Babe Ruth to the Yankees; it ended with a 4-0 sweep of the Cardinals in 2004 after beating the Yankees in the ALCS following a 0-3 deficit.
