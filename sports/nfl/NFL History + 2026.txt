LEAGUE HISTORY, FRANCHISES AND FAMOUS GAMES
League formed in Canton, Ohio in 1920 as American Professional Football Association (APFA); renamed National Football League on June 24, 1922. First league president: Jim Thorpe. First champions: Akron Pros, 1920.
Chicago Bears originated as Decatur Staleys (1920), moved to Chicago as Staleys in 1921, became Bears in 1922. George Halas was player-coach and owner.
Detroit Lions were Portsmouth Spartans before moving to Detroit and adopting Lions name in 1934.
First NFL draft: 1936. First overall selection: Jay Berwanger, Philadelphia Eagles; he never played in the NFL. First drafted player to sign: Riley Smith, second pick.
Fritz Pollard of Akron Pros became the league's first Black head coach in 1921.
First 1,000-yard rusher: Beattie Feathers, Chicago Bears, 1934 (1,004 yards).
Green Bay Packers founded 1919, joined league 1921. Most NFL championships including pre-Super Bowl titles: Packers 13; Super Bowl wins four. Green Bay nickname: Titletown.
Oldest continuously operating pro football franchise: Arizona Cardinals, founded 1898 as Morgan Athletic Club. Earlier locations/names: Chicago Cardinals, St. Louis Cardinals (1960-1987), Phoenix Cardinals (1988-1993), Arizona Cardinals (1994 onward).
Only perfect NFL season including playoffs in the Super Bowl era: 1972 Miami Dolphins, 17-0 (14 regular-season wins, three playoff wins), coached by Don Shula; won Super Bowl VII 14-7 vs Washington.
2007 New England Patriots went 16-0 regular season, 18-0 before Super Bowl XLII, then lost 17-14 to New York Giants; finished 18-1. This was not a perfect full season.
Immaculate Reception: Franco Harris caught a deflected Terry Bradshaw pass for Pittsburgh's winning TD vs Oakland Raiders in 1972 AFC divisional playoff; Steelers won 13-7.
Ice Bowl: 1967 NFL Championship, December 31 at Lambeau Field; Green Bay Packers beat Dallas Cowboys 21-17; Bart Starr scored winning QB sneak; Vince Lombardi coached Green Bay.
Greatest Game Ever Played: 1958 NFL Championship, Baltimore Colts beat New York Giants 23-17 in sudden-death overtime at Yankee Stadium. Alan Ameche scored winning TD; Johnny Unitas was Colts QB.
The Drive: John Elway led Denver Broncos 98 yards to tie Cleveland Browns in 1986 AFC Championship played January 11, 1987; Mark Jackson caught tying TD; Denver won 23-20 in overtime.
The Comeback (1992 season, played January 3, 1993): Frank Reich led Buffalo Bills from 32 points behind Houston Oilers to a playoff victory. This is a playoff landmark, not necessarily the current all-games comeback record.
